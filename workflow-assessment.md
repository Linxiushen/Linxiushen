# A focused AI workflow assessment

**One recurring task. Two main systems. A clear implementation decision.**

I help ecommerce operations and creative teams in Europe and North America connect existing AI and office tools and turn the process into something their team can run. Delivery is remote, in English, directly with an independent consultant.

The starting point is a paid assessment of one workflow. We identify the handoffs, access constraints and evidence needed to decide whether an implementation is worth doing.

## What the assessment covers

- One workflow, one business owner and up to two named systems.
- Up to five representative examples supplied with permission; redacted examples and a screen-share can be enough to begin.
- A 45-minute working session and a 30-minute decision call.
- One consolidated factual correction round.

You receive four practical deliverables:

| Deliverable | What it lets you do |
|---|---|
| Workflow map | See inputs, steps, tools, people, handoffs and the required output. |
| Evidence sheet | Distinguish observed facts, estimates and unknowns; identify what to measure. |
| Feasibility note | Understand available access and constraints in two systems, manual steps and technical checks still needed. |
| Decision and pilot outline | Choose to build, adjust or stop, with a proposed boundary, dependencies and acceptance checks. |

The assessment can stand on its own. You can use the findings with your team or another provider. Implementation, live production integration and third-party software charges are outside this assessment scope.

## A possible first workflow

Product-image coordination: approved product information and references, an agreed design tool, human review and a destination for accepted work. This is an example to scope together, not a claim that these steps suit every company.

If the assessment supports implementation, we define a separate pilot: one workflow, up to two named connections, one environment and up to five users. Each connection specifies the permitted operations. A company-specific Codex Skill can capture reusable instructions and references; the required tools and access are configured and evaluated as part of the implementation.

The pilot includes agreed quality checks, failure and retry handling, operating documentation and a handover exercise. Subsequent improvements and team enablement receive a separate monthly allocation.
